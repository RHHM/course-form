Course Specification Builder

Describe a 25-session course — each session 90 minutes, 75 planned and 15 held back for you. This produces the specification file the generator reads. It is the only thing the model is given about your course.

    The course

    Start here. Seven steps, and it saves as you go, so you can leave and come back. If you would rather see a finished specification first, loading the example fills every step with a real course — edit over it or clear it and start again.

    Used to choose teaching methods suited to your field.

    Rigor

    This changes the output more than anything else here. It sets where readings come from, how much scaffolding a task carries, and whether a session may end with a question still open.

    Pitching below the room is the more common and more costly error. An under-pitched session ends early with nothing left to do, and students stop preparing once they learn the preparation wasn't needed.

    Units and topics

    The generator paces these across 25 sessions. Active-learning sessions typically carry one or two topics, so aim for roughly 15–40 topics in total.

    Learning outcomes

    Used verbatim — never rewritten or renumbered. The generator checks each one is advanced by at least two sessions and assessed by at least one assignment, and reports the gaps rather than inventing content to fill them.

    An outcome names something a student can be seen to do.

    "Understand game theory" cannot be marked; "identify the assumption a result depends on and construct a case where it fails" can. As you write, suggestions appear under each one — they are advice, never a block, and an outcome you are happy with can stay exactly as it is. The criteria come from Stanford's guide to creating learning outcomes.

    Assignments

    Leave the due session blank and the generator proposes a placement, marked as proposed. Weights are optional here; they appear in the syllabus if you give them.

    Class participation

    A grading bucket for in-class performance, assessed however you see fit. It is deliberately not an assignment: it appears in the syllabus grading table and changes nothing about how sessions or lesson plans are built.

    How you teach

    All optional. Leave the methods unselected and the generator picks ones suited to your discipline — a literature seminar and a topology class shouldn't run the same way.

    In-class methods

    Comma-separated. Named methods are used as given — the generator infers what they mean in your field.

    Where the class meets

    Sessions are written for the live online platform unless you say otherwise — instant breakout rooms, shared whiteboards, and polls that can be multiple-choice or long-form, run individually or inside a group. Plans treat it as a normal class with extra tools, not a diminished one.

    Session numbers between 1 and 25, comma-separated. Leave blank if the whole course is online. Those sessions are written for a standard classroom — whiteboards, a projector, desks and movable chairs — and the facilitator script changes accordingly.

    Only if you want coding workbooks.

    Required texts students must obtain

    Including paid ones. These are trusted as given and never verified — you know what you assigned. The generator may assign chapters from them but will never introduce a text that costs students money on its own.

    Journals you know are free to read

    Only for journals the open-access databases get wrong. Some journals are given away by their publisher but carry no open-access licence, so every database reports them as paywalled and their articles get rejected. The Journal of Economic Perspectives is the clearest case — free to anyone with a browser, invisible to every index.

    Each one is checked before it is believed: articles are sampled from across the journal's run and tested for a freely downloadable copy. A journal that passes is remembered for every future course, yours and your colleagues'. One that fails is reported and not used. You do not need to list ordinary open-access journals — those already work.

    Treated as authoritative about your intentions — it overrides the generator's generic defaults where the two conflict. In testing this field changed the output more than any other optional one.
